# Calculus I — Test 1 demo package

## Demo scenario
A student in Calculus I (201-NYA-05) is preparing for their first test using the uploaded lecture slides. The app should generate a source-grounded study guide, quiz the student, identify weak topics from their answers, and create targeted follow-up practice.

## Source files (original PDFs, unchanged)
- Lecture1.pdf — Functions and function representations
- Lecture2.pdf — Limits, infinity and continuity
- Lecture3-4.pdf — Limit laws, indeterminate forms, trigonometric limits and continuity
- Lecture5.pdf — Introduction to differentiation and the derivative at a point
- Lecture6.pdf — Differentiability and the derivative as a function
- Lecture7.pdf — Basic differentiation rules and higher derivatives
- Lecture8.pdf — Product and quotient rules
- Lecture9.pdf — Composition of functions and chain rule

These are 8 PDF files representing lectures 1–9 (lectures 3 and 4 are combined). Do not assume the instructor's actual Test 1 covered every listed topic: the student specified these are the lectures to use for the demo.

## Suggested live demonstration
1. Select Calculus I and upload the 8 lecture PDFs (or pre-load them if uploads are slow).
2. Request: 'Create a Test 1 study guide based only on these uploaded lectures. Group by topic, explain important concepts, and cite the source lecture for each topic.'
3. Request: 'Generate 5 practice questions from these lectures with a mix of limits and derivatives. Do not reveal answers until I submit.'
4. Answer a chain-rule question incorrectly on purpose.
5. Show the weakness tracker flagging the relevant topic (if implemented).
6. Request targeted follow-up questions about that topic, with explanations and lecture references.

## Important testing notes
- Do not use Circuit Analysis 1 materials with this demo: these are Calculus I documents.
- Check that generated questions match the uploaded lectures and that calculations are correct.
- For each wrong AI answer, capture the question, AI answer, expected correction, and source lecture/page if possible.
- A student getting one answer wrong is a weak signal, not proof of a permanent weakness.
- The PDFs may contain formulas, graphs and image-based content: test whether extraction preserves them.
